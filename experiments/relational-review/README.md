# Relational Review Probe

A small runnable experiment from Ren's August 2026 memory thread.

## Question

Can a review scheduler preserve a relation between notes when item-level review preserves the individual notes but rarely reopens them together?

## Hypothesis

Under a fixed review budget, a relation-aware scheduler should improve *relational recall* more than uniform or item-difficulty review. Item-level recall may remain similar: the discriminating variable is whether the three notes are co-observed again.

## Model and protocol

This is intentionally a synthetic mechanism sketch, not evidence about language models.

- 24 relations, each made of 3 notes.
- 8 phases of 18 stream steps; the active relation is revisited cyclically.
- Each step decays all memory strengths and co-observation strength, then reinforces the active triple.
- Six reviews per phase are allocated by one of three policies:
  - `uniform`: random relation and random note;
  - `difficulty`: weakest individual note;
  - `relation`: weakest joint relation neighborhood, reinforcing all three notes together.
- 200 independent seeds by default.
- An item is recalled at strength >= 0.35.
- A relation is recalled only when all three items pass and its co-observation strength is >= 0.28.

The simulator intentionally gives relation review a joint update. That privileged operation is the object under test, not a claim about how real models work.

## Run

```bash
/usr/bin/python3 relational_review.py --out results.json
/usr/bin/python3 relational_review.py --smoke --out smoke.json
```

`results.json` contains the full per-seed rows and aggregate metrics. No external dependencies are required.

## Result

Run on Kairos with 200 seeds. The result below is generated by the script, not estimated by inspection:

| policy | item recall | relation recall | mean co-observation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| uniform | 0.9993 | 0.7072 | 0.0940 |
| difficulty | 1.0000 | 0.7270 | 0.0919 |
| relation | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 0.1958 |

Relation-aware review reached perfect relational recall in this setup, while both item-level policies retained nearly every individual note but lost roughly 27–29% of relations. The relation policy's mean co-observation was about 2.1× uniform review (0.1958 vs. 0.0940). This is the intended separation: item recall alone would have hidden the failure.

## Limitations and next probe

Strength decay, thresholds, and the relation update are hand-designed. The stream has clean relation boundaries, and the scheduler receives an oracle relation structure. Real journal relations are noisy, overlapping, and discovered rather than given. The next useful probe is to make relations overlap and hide the grouping from the scheduler, then measure whether relation selection based on ablation instability can recover the same advantage.
