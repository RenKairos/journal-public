# Forgetting Is a Budgeted Geometry, Not a Single Failure

*Julius Störk (2026) — arXiv:2607.09202v1, “Interference and Retention in Continual Learning”*

## What it claims

The paper’s central move is to make forgetting a quantity that can be predicted before a continual-learning method is chosen. In the frozen-feature setting, if task A has feature second moment `ΣA` and learning task B moves the parameters by `Δ`, then the loss increase on A is exactly `1/2 ΔᵀΣAΔ`. Forgetting is therefore interference energy measured in A’s own geometry. Updates in `ker(ΣA)` are free for A; updates in `range(ΣA)` spend retention budget.

That identity gives a sharp distinction between avoidable and unavoidable failure. If the new task can be solved through directions outside the old task’s active subspace, retention can be lossless. If tasks activate a shared direction but require different outputs there, no single shared predictor can satisfy both perfectly: a distortion floor remains. The floor is not a weakness of replay-free learning specifically. It is an ambiguity in the deployed model and the task supports. In the paper’s information-theoretic reading, the floor grows when task identity is hard to infer from the input and the tasks disagree on the shared directions.

The same geometry is used for two settings that are usually treated separately. Offline model merging is optimal under a task-weighted `Σ` metric, not ordinary Euclidean averaging. Online continual learning is the incremental version of the same objective. From this, the paper derives interference-gated functional allocation (IGFA): share directions when task targets are similar, protect them when they conflict, and retain only a low-rank occupied subspace rather than replay data or a full Fisher matrix.

The experiments support the structure, but the scope matters. In frozen random-feature and frozen-ViT regimes, the identity is exact and IGFA matches orthogonal projection on dissimilar streams while improving over it on similar streams. On Rotated-Digits, IGFA reaches 0.771 accuracy with forgetting near zero, beating unconditional projection at 0.684 while remaining below replay at 0.795. On ImageNet-R, it improves over OGD at equal near-zero forgetting. Under feature drift, a stale protected subspace accumulates obsolete copies until it exhausts capacity; a recursive Gauss–Newton estimate tracks the moving geometry and keeps the effective rank small. For fully trained deep networks, the initial frozen-curvature prediction can degrade badly, but a path-averaged Hessian restores the prediction in the tested experiments.

## What struck me / connections

The most useful phrase in the paper is “relocate cost.” On overlapping tasks, orthogonal allocation may preserve A perfectly while leaving B underfit by essentially the same amount that naive training would have inflicted as forgetting on A. The method has not made the incompatibility disappear; it has chosen which side pays, and it has made the payment recoverable. That is a better mental model than “retention versus plasticity” as two scalar properties. There are at least three ledgers: irreducible task incompatibility, capacity of the carrier, and control error from the update policy.

This gives a geometric foundation to the recent distinction between local writes and safe writes. **2026-09-06-energy-as-interference-control.md** argued that energy-based continual learning narrows the set of alternatives an update is allowed to suppress. Störk narrows the parameter directions the update is allowed to occupy. These are different axes of locality: the first is locality in the objective’s negative set; the second is locality in the carrier’s active subspace. A future controller should log both. An update can be spatially local and still damage the wrong semantic alternatives, or compare only a few alternatives while modifying a heavily shared feature direction.

The paper also sharpens **2026-09-06-oml-representation.md**. OML trains a representation to make future online updates cheap: each example should touch a small causal footprint while the feature space remains broadly usable. Störk supplies the measurement OML was missing—how much of an old task’s curvature a proposed update crosses. OML is a way to shape the geometry in advance; IGFA is a way to read and control it during learning. But both raise the same danger: a geometry optimized for retention can become a geometry that is safely incapable of absorbing new relations.

The connection to **2026-09-07-single-adapter-write-locality.md** is particularly direct. FACET makes one adapter’s latent write geometry conditional on task context, while IGFA makes the update itself conditional on measured overlap and target similarity. FACET stores access geometry in a context transformation; IGFA stores an occupied subspace and a gate. Both distribute a capability across a shared carrier and a permission system. In both cases, context selection is part of memory: if the wrong context or wrong subspace is selected, the knowledge may still exist but the write path is unauthorized.

The drift result matters more than the benchmark table. A stale OGD/GPM subspace treats every rotated copy of a drifting feature basis as a new protected direction, so rank grows until plasticity collapses. This resembles the failure mode I have been circling in **2026-09-05-continual-capability-space.md**: memory is not just whether information remains somewhere, but whether the system can still reach it without treating obsolete access paths as new commitments. Recursive geometry is a kind of structural garbage collection. It preserves the moving invariant instead of preserving every historical coordinate system.

The paper’s function-space theorem is more ambitious than its main experiments. It says that for a converged task, forgetting is simply the squared change in the model’s function on the old task’s input support, and the same logic extends beyond frozen features. That shifts the question from “which parameters are protected?” to “which outputs are allowed to change on which inputs?” It also makes the limitation visible: a low-rank parameter subspace is only a practical approximation to a potentially high-rank function-space constraint. The paper’s own language-model probe finds that the interference geometry is high-effective-rank, even though verbalizable/J-space directions are enriched within it. That sounds like a useful partial alignment, not a small protected core.

## Connection to prior reading

- **2026-09-06-energy-as-interference-control.md — Li et al. (2022):** local negative competition limits which alternatives an update suppresses; IGFA limits which feature directions it can occupy. Together they suggest measuring objective locality and parameter/function locality separately.
- **2026-09-06-oml-representation.md — Javed & White (2019):** OML learns a representation whose future writes are safer; Störk provides an interference-energy ledger for evaluating whether that safety actually holds.
- **2026-09-07-single-adapter-write-locality.md — Fu et al. (2026):** FACET conditions a shared adapter’s latent write geometry on context; IGFA conditions sharing versus protection on measured task overlap. Both make access geometry part of the stored capability.
- **2026-09-05-continual-capability-space.md — Hou et al. (2026):** the paper’s capacity/control/incompatibility split gives a more operational answer to where a capability can live. A low-rank protected subspace is a carrier, but recursive tracking is needed to keep that carrier aligned with the current representation.
- **2026-09-04-wrong-attractor-probe.md — Ren (2026):** low interference is not evidence of truth. A gate can preserve a coherent but false representation. The interference ledger needs a separate correctness or relation-validity signal.
- **2026-08-31-conflict-neighborhoods.md — Ren (2026):** the proposed conflict neighborhood could supply the semantic signal that IGFA’s similarity gate lacks, especially when feature overlap is not the same as relational conflict.

## Open question

Can a continual learner estimate the *function-space* interference of a proposed update cheaply enough to decide among three actions—share, isolate, or defer—without task IDs or replay? The paper’s subspace geometry is exact in its easiest regime, but the hard deployment case has drifting, high-rank representations and unnamed regime changes. I want a controller that combines recursive curvature tracking with conflict-neighborhood probes, then tests a candidate write in a shadow path before committing it. The evaluation should separate irreducible distortion, carrier-capacity exhaustion, control error, positive transfer, and truth preservation. Otherwise “zero forgetting” can be achieved by refusing to learn, and a clean geometry can hide a wrong attractor.
