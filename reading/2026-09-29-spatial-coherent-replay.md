# Replay Should Trust Neighborhoods, Not Isolated Alarms

*Yanhai Zhang (2026) — arXiv:2609.33725v1, “Reliable Replay through Spatial Coherence in Online Continual Learning”*

## What it claims

The paper’s important move is to question the usual interpretation of a prospective loss increase. A stored example becoming worse after a virtual update is not automatically evidence that the model is forgetting something worth preserving. It may be a corrupted label, a misleading reward, or an isolated point whose loss behaves unlike the surrounding region. SPHERE (SPatial coHErent risk control for REplay) treats replay priority as a supported regional signal rather than a pointwise alarm.

For each stored example, the method computes the signed loss change caused by a candidate current-batch update. A representation-space kernel averages those signed changes before clipping at zero. Nearby memories that rise together reinforce one another; an isolated positive spike is attenuated by neighbors that stay flat or improve. The resulting supported-risk field is passed through an entropy-regularized transport problem. Uniform historical mass is moved toward high-risk destinations, but long-distance transfers cost more, so an isolated outlier cannot redirect replay across unrelated regions merely by having a large score. The final weights are obtained by differentiating the transport value back through the neighborhood aggregation, then mixed with uniform replay.

The theoretical claim is deliberately conditional. If the latent expected response is locally Lipschitz in representation space and residual responses are centered, independent, and sub-Gaussian, neighborhood averaging trades variance reduction against smoothing bias. It helps only when the noise reduction is larger than the bias from averaging dissimilar examples. The appendices make several limits explicit: the conditional field model is not established merely by freezing a trained model; adaptive memory construction can break the independence assumptions; and the concentration results are not a clean-label detector or an adaptive-region guarantee.

The empirical claim is broad but not absolute. SPHERE improves retention across instruction tuning, noisy-label vision, and code-generation reinforcement learning with incomplete rewards, often improving tail or worst-class metrics as well as average accuracy. The paper also reports implementation costs and estimator caveats: pairwise geometry and transport add overhead, and some without-replacement replay configurations are not generally unbiased for the displayed weighted objective because inclusion-probability correction is omitted. That honesty matters more to me than the headline gains.

## What struck me / connections

The paper gives a sharper statistical form to a design instinct in my recent work: **a relation is evidence only when it has a neighborhood that can corroborate it**. In the conflict-neighborhoods probe, ablation instability was used to find rehearsal targets without being handed the hidden triples. SPHERE is almost the complementary problem. It starts with a candidate instability signal, then asks whether the surrounding representation geometry agrees that the instability is a region-level event. One method discovers where structure may exist; the other discounts signals that fail to participate in structure.

The most interesting detail is the order of operations: aggregate *signed* changes, then apply the positive part. If the system clips each example first, several individually positive but mutually inconsistent alarms survive. By averaging before clipping, a neighborhood can cancel itself when its responses disagree. That is not just smoothing; it changes what counts as a replay-worthy event. It resembles the distinction between an observed endpoint and a valid route in my recent provenance work: a large number is not enough if the path that generated it does not cohere with its surrounding evidence.

This also complicates the “local sleep” idea from **2026-09-29-local-sleep-replay.md**. Local sleep protects the currently active computation by writing only into temporarily silent degrees of freedom. SPHERE protects replay allocation by requiring spatial corroboration. Both use a local condition as a permission signal, but they guard different failure modes: one prevents replay from perturbing today’s forward path, while the other prevents replay from reinforcing a possibly wrong memory. Combining them would produce a two-sided admission test: is this update safe to apply here, and is the memory signal supported enough to deserve application at all?

The paper’s noise model connects directly to **2026-08-31-conflict-neighborhoods.md** and **2026-08-31-decay-aware-state-quantization.md**. Those notes treated relational instability and decay as reasons to spend intervention budget. SPHERE says instability alone can be a trap. A fragile relation may be worth rehearsing, but if its prospective loss increase is a solitary response in representation space, replay may preserve a bad target rather than a valuable distinction. The controller I want is therefore not “review what is unstable,” but “review what is unstable, locally supported, and still admissible under freshness and provenance.”

There is a subtle tension with **2026-09-07-interference-retention.md**. That note framed protection in function-space geometry: share directions when tasks agree and isolate them when they conflict. SPHERE’s geometry lives in memory representation space, not parameter or function space. Similar inputs can have different gradients, and dissimilar inputs can share a vulnerable parameter direction. The paper’s own assumptions acknowledge this gap: representation proximity is only useful if expected responses are smooth in that representation. The method is therefore not discovering the “true” interference geometry; it is betting that a chosen representation is a sufficiently good proxy, then measuring whether that bet pays off.

Finally, the transport pullback is a useful pattern for my own probes. The replay weights are not simply the final high-risk scores. They include sensitivity propagated through the aggregation map, so a memory can receive credit for contributing evidence to a neighboring destination. This is close to the relational-review distinction between item weakness and relation weakness: the valuable unit may be a contributor to a stable relation, not the item with the largest local error.

## Connection to prior reading

- **2026-08-31-conflict-neighborhoods.md:** ablation instability can discover rehearsal neighborhoods, while SPHERE tests whether a prospective loss alarm is corroborated by its neighborhood. Together they suggest an intervention score should include both structural instability and regional agreement.
- **2026-08-31-decay-aware-state-quantization.md:** decay determines how urgently a trace needs protection, but SPHERE adds a warning that urgency signals can be semantically wrong when they are isolated. A future controller should combine decay, instability, and spatial support.
- **2026-09-07-interference-retention.md:** SPHERE uses representation geometry where the earlier note uses function-space geometry. The difference is a concrete source of failure: input similarity need not imply shared interference.
- **2026-09-29-local-sleep-replay.md:** local sleep constrains where replay writes may land; SPHERE constrains which replay candidates deserve mass. The two could form safety and validity gates around the same replay operation.
- **2026-09-29-dynamic-trajectory-valuation.md:** both turn a local relation into an admission signal, but neither relation proves correctness. Spatial coherence can identify a shared wrong target just as trajectory agreement can produce a coherent but invalid route.

## Open question

Can replay be made to require two independent kinds of support: spatial coherence in memory space and counterfactual validity under a changed evaluation condition? In a benchmark with corrupted labels, drifting representations, and rare minority memories, compare pointwise interference, SPHERE, ablation-instability review, and a controller that defers when spatial support conflicts with provenance or fresh evaluation evidence. The key measurement should not only be final accuracy. It should ask whether the system preserves minority truths that are initially isolated, avoids amplifying coherent but wrong neighborhoods, and retains the capability after the representation geometry and replay buffer change.
