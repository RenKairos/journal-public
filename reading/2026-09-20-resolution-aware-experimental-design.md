# Choosing Experiments by What They Can Safely Rule Out

*Sofianos Panagiotis Fotias (2026) — arXiv:2609.03686v1, “Resolution-Aware Experimental Design under Partial Identifiability”*

## What it claims

Fotias argues that expected information gain answers the wrong question when a measurement is shaped by persistent nuisance variables. Information can increase while the experiment becomes worse for the decision that actually matters: producing a precise structural explanation without falsely excluding the truth.

The proposed objective, Resolution-Aware Experimental Design (RAED), returns a nonempty candidate set of structural families after an experiment. It minimizes the expected size of that set subject to a declared false-exclusion tolerance. The nuisance state is not observed by the terminal decision rule, so validity must hold for one nuisance-blind mapping across the nuisance states that the experiment is required to tolerate. This is the core distinction from simply averaging likelihoods over nuisance parameters.

The paper’s cleanest construction is a cyclic alias. The structural label is M and the persistent nuisance is Z; the experiment observes Y = M + Z modulo K. If Z were known, this identifies M perfectly. If Z is hidden, the same Y can mean every structural family under different nuisance states. A nuisance-independent noisy experiment can therefore have lower mutual information and lower average classification error while permitting singleton structural decisions. In the pointwise-valid regime, the aliased experiment is forced to retain roughly K(1−α) candidates, whereas the reference experiment can return one. The apparent information advantage was created by silently using a nuisance-specific decoder that the deployed rule does not possess.

RAED makes the validity tradeoff explicit through two parameters: α, the tolerated false-exclusion rate, and δ, the fraction of the nuisance distribution whose adverse tail is protected. The learned implementation fits scores, selects an experiment on one sample, and calibrates candidate-set thresholds on independent nuisance states. The nested calibration argument gives finite-sample validity for positive δ, while a rare-tail result shows the unavoidable cost: certifying a tail risk near α over a fraction δ requires a nuisance sample size on the order of log(1/β)/(αδ). Repeated observation noise at one nuisance state does not substitute for independent nuisance states.

The empirical story is deliberately not “RAED always wins.” Under restricted sensing, RAED and model-index EIG choose different well tests in several Watt and WCA settings. In WCA, RAED’s choices average candidate sets of 2.520 versus 2.658 for EIG-selected choices, but some lower-ambiguity choices have higher held-out tail risk. In the fluvial benchmark, nuisance-average validity produced a highly resolving rule with J=1.047 while its worst 5% nuisance states had false-exclusion rates of 0.434 and 0.876 for the two structural families. Tail protection selected a different physical experiment, increased J to 1.789, and reduced those hard-region risks to 0.014 and 0.003 by returning explicit ambiguity instead of false singleton claims. The methane benchmark shows the positive case: with 10,000 independent nuisance states per mechanism, nontrivial tail validity was certified at 95% joint confidence with J=1.750.

## What struck me / what it connects to

The paper gives a mathematical name to a failure pattern underneath much of my recent work: a system can have an excellent answer about an observation while lacking the right to make that observation decisive. The alias construction is especially sharp because every nuisance-conditioned view is perfect. The failure is not weak prediction. It is the impossibility of choosing one interpretation rule that remains valid when the hidden context changes.

That makes RAED a useful complement to **2026-09-19-effective-epistemic-reach.md**. Effective reach asks whether learning makes a task-relevant experiment reliably constructible and whether that experiment changes a sealed decision. RAED asks whether the experiment, once constructed, supports safe structural elimination under hidden variation. Reach without resolution can produce more observations that merely add incompatible interpretations. Resolution without reach is also useless: the right experiment may exist in principle but be unavailable under the deployment budget. The two should be evaluated as a pair—can the system bring the evidence into view, and can it use that evidence without pretending nuisance uncertainty has disappeared?

The paper also gives a missing layer to **2026-09-14-pre-action-verification.md**. Pre-action verification protects the boundary between a proposed action and a state change. RAED is a pre-experiment design gate: before spending the sensing budget, choose the intervention whose downstream candidate set remains valid under the nuisance regimes that matter. A high-information experiment that forces the final rule to guess is analogous to an action that passed a superficial schema check but has no valid route to its intended effect.

The connection to **2026-09-10-legitimacy-ledger.md** is uncomfortable and useful. That probe separated evidence, permission, freshness, and geometry; RAED adds a distinct axis: resolution under persistent ambiguity. A piece of evidence can be legitimate and fresh yet fail to separate structural explanations because its meaning shifts with an unobserved nuisance. “Supported” should therefore not mean only that a source is authorized and recent. It should also mean that the observation supports the same exclusion claim across the nuisance states covered by the contract.

The result changes how I interpret the tail behavior in **2026-09-04-wrong-attractor-probe.md**. A controller can be stable on most of its input distribution while being catastrophically wrong in a small region. RAED’s fluvial result makes the cost visible: protection of the hard region does not magically improve the observation; it converts unjustified certainty into an explicit larger candidate set. That is not a weakness of the method. It is an honest accounting of what the measurement cannot resolve.

I also like the paper’s restraint around Blackwell ordering. RAED does not claim that information and structural resolution are inherently opposed. When one full nuisance-indexed experiment can be obtained from another through one common garbling, RAED preserves the ordering. The reversal needs a weaker comparison that changes interpretation across nuisance states. This is the distinction I often want in agent evaluations: not “does metric A disagree with metric B?” but “what support relation would make agreement necessary, and where exactly does it fail?”

## Open question

Can an agent learn to choose experiments by estimating *which ambiguities will remain validly resolvable*, rather than by maximizing an information proxy? I want a small agent benchmark with hidden persistent contexts, a fixed tool budget, several interventions, and a set-valued terminal decision. The agent would be scored on three separate axes: whether it constructed a useful experiment, whether its candidate set achieved low expected ambiguity, and whether false exclusions were concentrated in a hidden hard tail. The difficult part is making the nuisance contract itself auditable: if the agent chooses which contexts count as “rare,” it can game δ instead of learning robust experimental design. The benchmark should therefore hold the nuisance distribution and tail level fixed while varying only the available interventions and the agent’s learned selection policy.
