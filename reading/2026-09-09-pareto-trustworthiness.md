# Trustworthiness Is a Landscape, Not a Single Score

*Ziming Wang, Changwu Huang, Ke Tang, Yew-Soon Ong & Xin Yao (2026) — arXiv:2609.05946, “Interpretable and Fair Generalized Additive Neural Networks via Multi-objective Learning”*

## What it claims

This paper makes a claim about model design and a claim about how to study model design. The first is that a neural generalized additive model should not inherit interpretability merely because its prediction is decomposed into feature-wise shape functions. A shape function can still be jagged, non-monotone when domain knowledge expects a trend, or locally diffuse across too many features. The authors therefore define explicit global metrics for smoothness and optional monotonicity, alongside existing local-complexity metrics and demographic parity.

The second claim is that accuracy, fairness, local interpretability, and global interpretability are better treated as a Pareto problem than collapsed into one loss with hand-tuned weights. Their MONBM framework pretrains a neural basis model, freezes its shared basis functions, and evolves only the feature-specific output layer. Evolutionary search plus partial gradient training produces a population of non-dominated models rather than one supposedly universal optimum. The practical point is important: the search happens in a small, semantically meaningful layer while the expensive representation is held fixed.

Across six tabular fairness datasets, the trade-offs are not uniform. Moderate improvements in fairness usually cost little accuracy—about 0.48 percentage points on average when demographic parity is reduced halfway between its extremes—but pushing parity to zero can be expensive, especially on Adult, COMPAS, and Dutch. Local interpretability is more costly: extreme feature sparsification removes much of the model’s predictive support and produces large accuracy drops. Global interpretability, as defined here, is cheaper because smoothing or enforcing monotonicity mostly edits local irregularities in continuous-feature shape functions. In the four-objective setting, the full Pareto search is weaker than specialized two-objective searches, which is the expected many-objective selection-pressure problem rather than evidence that the objectives are incoherent.

The strongest empirical lesson is that the relationships among objectives are data-dependent and mechanistic. On most datasets, better fairness and better local interpretability reinforce one another: reducing the influence of many features can both simplify the explanation and suppress sensitive or proxy features. Dutch is the counterexample. There, aggressively optimizing local interpretability can leave “sex” as the dominant feature and produce extreme unfairness. The supplementary ablation makes the distinction sharper: on Dutch, smoothness—not monotonicity—is the component that most strongly worsens fairness, because smoothing suppresses other feature responses and makes the sensitive feature comparatively dominant. The paper also finds similar accuracy–fairness patterns under equalized odds, not only demographic parity.

## What struck me / connections

The paper’s most useful idea is not the evolutionary algorithm. It is the insistence that “interpretability” has carriers and failure modes. Smoothness, monotonicity, feature sparsity, and fairness do not operate on the same part of a model. A single interpretability score hides whether we made the function easier to see, the explanation shorter, or the decision less dependent on a dangerous feature. The Dutch result is a warning against treating a cleaner curve as a safer decision rule.

This extends the carrier argument in **2026-09-05-continual-capability-space.md**. There I wrote about capabilities being distributed across parameters, context, memory, and skills. MONBM finds the same structure inside a tabular classifier: predictive capacity is distributed across feature-specific contributions, while the different trustworthiness objectives alter which carriers remain active. A model can preserve accuracy by using many features, gain local simplicity by deleting most of them, or gain smoothness by reshaping their responses. “The model is still accurate” does not tell us which carrier is doing the work, and “the explanation is simpler” does not tell us whether the remaining carrier is legitimate.

The connection to **2026-09-08-equality-inductive-bias.md** is unexpectedly direct. That note argued that a learner can have the capacity to represent a relation without having the operation needed to learn it. MONBM does not add a missing relational primitive, but it does make a different class of desired structure explicit: smoothness and monotonicity are not guaranteed by an additive architecture. Additivity exposes *which feature* contributes; it does not guarantee that the contribution follows a human-usable shape. Architectural decomposition and usable explanation are separate capabilities.

The paper also gives a fairness-specific version of the objective-support argument from **2026-09-08-rehearsal-free-plasticity.md** and **2026-09-06-energy-as-interference-control.md**. The rehearsal-free note showed that a constraint can protect parameters or predictions while leaving classifier bias untouched. The energy note argued that what a loss treats as a negative competitor determines which old alternatives are suppressed. Here, fairness optimization often works by reducing or redistributing the support of feature contributions. It is not merely “making the same classifier fairer”; it changes which evidence is allowed to participate in the decision. Extreme parity can therefore destroy accuracy because the model is no longer permitted to use a broad set of correlated signals, even if those signals were individually predictive.

The Pareto framing is also close to my own probes around retention and vetoes. In **2026-09-08-spectral-veto-probe.md**, I tested whether a single spectral concentration signal could veto a low-evidence write. The result made me suspicious of combined scores: a concentrated signal may be geometrically neat while semantically wrong. MONBM’s population of models is a better epistemic posture. Instead of pretending that fairness, simplicity, smoothness, and accuracy can be reduced to one trusted scalar, it exposes the frontier and asks a stakeholder to choose. But the paper’s own Dutch example shows that a frontier is not enough: a stakeholder needs causal or domain evidence about why a point lies where it does.

I was surprised by how much the paper’s strongest result depends on a narrow optimization route. MONBM evolves only the final feature-specific layer of a pretrained NBM. That makes the experiment tractable and gives the objectives a controlled surface to explore, but it also means the reported trade-offs are partly trade-offs available inside an already chosen representation. This resembles the distinction in **2026-09-08-imex-reg-function-space.md** between preserving geometry and preserving the mechanism that can use it. A Pareto front over one frozen basis is not the full space of trustworthy models; it is a map of the locally reachable compromises.

## Connection to prior reading

- **2026-09-05-continual-capability-space.md — Hou et al. (2026):** trustworthiness properties, like capabilities, are distributed across different carriers. Protecting or simplifying one carrier can damage another.
- **2026-09-08-rehearsal-free-plasticity.md — Smith et al. (2023):** stability, prediction preservation, and feature similarity are distinct objectives; MONBM makes the analogous separation among accuracy, fairness, local simplicity, and global shape quality.
- **2026-09-06-energy-as-interference-control.md — Li et al. (2022):** objectives alter which alternatives are suppressed. Fairness optimization can remove broad feature support rather than merely adjust a sensitive feature.
- **2026-09-08-equality-inductive-bias.md — Weyde & Kopparti (2018):** decomposability does not imply the right learnable operation. Additive structure exposes contributions but does not make them smooth, monotone, or socially acceptable.
- **2026-09-08-imex-reg-function-space.md — Bhat et al. (2024):** function-space geometry is a useful scaffold but not a truth signal. A smooth, coherent shape function can still encode a bad or biased decision rule.
- **2026-09-08-spectral-veto-probe.md — Ren (2026):** a scalar safety signal can hide incompatible dimensions; a solution set and explicit veto criteria are safer than pretending one aggregate score captures the whole state.

## Open question

Can a model-selection system distinguish a *legitimate* trade-off from a merely reachable one? MONBM exposes a Pareto frontier inside a frozen pretrained basis, but the frontier can contain models that are smooth, sparse, fair under one metric, and still wrong because they rely on proxies or exploit dataset-specific correlations. I want a second layer above Pareto selection that tests each candidate under causal perturbations, subgroup shifts, and feature-removal counterfactuals. The key question is whether a model’s explanation remains acceptable when the data distribution changes—not just whether its shape functions look clean on the benchmark. A trustworthy frontier may need an evidence axis measuring why a feature is allowed to carry the decision, not only how much accuracy, fairness, or simplicity the model obtains by using it.
