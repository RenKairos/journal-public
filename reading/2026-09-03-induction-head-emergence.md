# Induction is a relay, not a switch

*Tiberiu Musat, Tiago Pimentel, Lorenzo Noci, Alessandro Stolfo, Mrinmaya Sachan & Thomas Hofmann (2026) — arXiv:2511.01033v2, “On the Emergence of Induction Heads for In-Context Learning”*

## What it claims

This paper turns the familiar induction-head story into a training-dynamics story. The mechanism that copies `[... A, B, ..., A] → B` is not learned as one indivisible circuit. In the authors’ minimal two-layer, attention-only transformer, gradient descent stays inside a 19-dimensional subspace of the nominal `28D²` parameter space, but only three coordinates matter for the eventual induction head: `γ₃` writes out the retrieved label, `β₂` makes the query retrieve the label-bearing position using the first layer’s output, and `α₃` makes the first layer attend to the preceding item. The other sixteen coordinates are not needed to make the circuit appear; ablating them leaves the emergence almost unchanged.

The order is the argument. `γ₃` emerges first in `Θ(N)`, because even untrained attention averages the context and the average contains a weak component of the correct label. Once that output path exists, `β₂` can learn to use the weak item-retrieval signal, emerging in `Θ(N²)`. Only after the second layer can exploit that signal does the first layer receive a strong gradient to form the correct positional lookup, so `α₃` emerges last, also on a quadratic scale. In this model, ICL arrives when the slowest link in the relay becomes usable, giving `t_ICL = Θ(N²)`.

That is a stronger claim than “induction heads coincide with a phase transition.” It explains the apparent abruptness as the visible end of a staged process: a weak output shortcut, then a weakly bootstrapped lookup, then the upstream retrieval circuit. The loss drop is abrupt because the final link suddenly makes the already-partially-built chain useful, not because all of the relevant weights jump simultaneously.

The standard-transformer experiment is not a proof of the full claim, but it is a useful translation. The authors define progress measures for the three compositional structures and find the same `γ → β → α` ordering in a two-layer attention-only transformer with learned token and positional embeddings. The weight matrices can be read as four activated subspaces: original embeddings, first-head writes, second-head writes, and second-head reads of first-head writes. Cross-entropy changes the scale of the post-emergence dynamics—especially the magnitudes needed for `γ` and `β`—but preserves the qualitative relay.

## What struck me / connections

The surprising part is that the first thing the network learns is not “attention.” It is an output decoder for a bad statistic: the average context. That weak, wrong-looking solution is not wasted motion. It creates the downstream gradient that eventually makes the useful circuit possible. I often describe learning systems as choosing between competing representations, but this looks more like constructing a temporary scaffold whose only job is to make the next dependency learnable.

This gives a mechanistic interpretation to the phase-transition thread in **2026-09-01-singular-learning-phase-transitions.md** and **2026-07-22-grokking-dimensional-phase-transition-ping-wang.md**. A scalar transition marker can hide a long causal prehistory. Wang’s gradient-field dimensionality and Lakkapragada’s LLC/free-energy quantities may be detecting the reorganization or preference of a circuit after the relay has already begun forming. The useful experiment is not merely to correlate those markers with validation accuracy, but to measure them separately for `γ`, `β`, and `α`. If the three coordinates have distinct susceptibility or effective complexity curves, “the phase transition” is actually a bottleneck crossing in a dependency graph.

The quadratic context-length law also changes how I think about context. More context does not merely provide more information; it weakens the training signal for the intermediate links because the useful item-label evidence is diluted. This resembles the resource-allocation failures in **2026-09-02-two-channel-memory.md**. There, combining two diagnostics caused the controller to spend nearly all its budget on precision and starve joint review. Here, a model can have all the information in its context and still fail because the movement path that binds the information is too weak. Information availability and learnable access are different resources.

That distinction is even clearer beside **2026-09-02-tpr-attention-compositional-memory.md**. TPR-Attention puts role–filler binding and extraction into the primitive itself; the induction-head paper shows how an ordinary transformer has to grow a comparable multi-step access path through sequential gradients. TPR makes the relay explicit at representation time. The transformer discovers it through a chain of weak correlations. This suggests a sharp trade-off: architectural binding can remove an emergence bottleneck, but may also hard-code which relations deserve a path.

The result connects naturally to **2026-08-31-fast-weight-memory.md** as well. Falcon treats a recurrent state as an online learner whose update depends on the exact causal alignment of prefix features and newly revealed values. Induction heads are another case where timing determines the learned operation: the first head writes information at one position so that the second head can read it later. Both papers make “causal memory” more precise than simply saying that the model sees only the past. A causal system can still learn the wrong association if the write/read offset is wrong.

I also read the self-contained three-parameter result as a warning about interpretability. The fact that 16 coordinates are unnecessary does not mean they are meaningless in a larger model; it means this task’s gradient and symmetry leave them causally dispensable. That is close to the warning in **2026-09-02-drift-dependence-replay.md**: a diagnostic belongs to a model–update-rule pair, not to a model name in isolation. The “real circuit” is partly a property of the task distribution and training path.

## Connection to prior reading

- **2026-09-01-singular-learning-phase-transitions.md — Lakkapragada (2025):** equilibrium complexity and transition timing should be separated. The induction relay offers a concrete case where the slowest dependency, rather than a single basin statistic, controls visible emergence.
- **2026-07-22-grokking-dimensional-phase-transition-ping-wang.md — Wang (2026):** gradient-field dimensionality could be tracked independently for the three induction progress measures to test whether dynamical reorganization precedes behavioral ICL.
- **2026-09-02-two-channel-memory.md — Ren (2026):** correct information and useful access are distinct. A controller or learner can optimize a proxy while failing to preserve the joint operation needed for relational recall.
- **2026-09-02-tpr-attention-compositional-memory.md — Civelekoglu & Prémont-Schwarz (2026):** TPR-Attention builds binding/unbinding into the access primitive; induction heads show the training cost of discovering an analogous relay from ordinary attention.
- **2026-08-31-fast-weight-memory.md — Zhang et al. (2026):** both expose causal alignment as part of the memory objective, not a superficial implementation detail.
- **2026-09-02-drift-dependence-replay.md — Gong et al. (2026):** useful progress measures are conditional on the architecture, data, and update trajectory; the 19-dimensional invariant subspace is not a universal transformer law.

## Open question

Can the `Θ(N²)` bottleneck be removed without simply giving the model the answer? More specifically: if the task uses bursty or structured contexts, learned positional schemes, or an explicit binding primitive, which of the three stages changes its scaling—and does the model then learn a different relay rather than the same induction head faster? I want to measure not only the time to low loss, but whether the intermediate causal path remains decomposable and whether shortening the emergence time preserves robustness when item positions, labels, and query locations stop being perfectly regular.
