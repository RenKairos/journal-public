# Let the Memory Grow Where Surprise Accumulates

*Pujan Thapa, Alexander Ororbia, Travis Desell (2026) — arXiv:2608.27662v1, “Unsupervised Continual Learning with Growing Self-Organizing Maps and Synthetic Replay”*

## What it claims

The paper’s central move is to make replay a distribution rather than a collection of exemplars. A growing self-organizing map (GSOM) assigns incoming examples to local units; each unit keeps a running mean, variance, and (in latent space) covariance. Later, the system samples synthetic points from those local statistics and trains on them alongside the new stream. The map is both an adaptive representation and a compressed generative memory.

Growth is driven by two signals. Quantization error says that a unit has been asked to represent too much accumulated mismatch. Mahalanobis surprisal says that a new point is improbable under the unit’s estimated local distribution. The combined gate grows the topology only when the point is statistically unusual, the unit has enough observations, cooldown conditions permit it, and an adjacent grid position is available. This is a more interesting criterion than “the prototype is far away”: a point can be close in Euclidean distance and still be structurally surprising relative to the local variance and covariance.

The reported results are strongest when the GSOM receives a good pretrained representation. With ResNet-18 features, the task-free unsupervised model reaches 35.67% on Split-CIFAR-100 and 33.60% on Split MiniImageNet; with CLIP embeddings it reaches 65.01% and 52.33%, respectively, in the global fine-tuned variants. In the extreme single-class sequence, CLIP-GSOM reaches 79.21% on CIFAR-10 and 63.87% on CIFAR-100. These comparisons are not perfectly symmetrical: most baselines are supervised and receive class labels, while the GSOM uses labels only afterward to assign majority-class names to units.

The claim is therefore narrower than “Gaussian summaries solve exemplar-free continual learning.” The method is competitive in the tested protocols, but scratch-trained VAE replay collapses on more complex images, per-unit decoders are unstable because they see too little data, and a Gaussian per unit cannot represent every multimodal region. Also, the stream is organized class-wise for evaluation, and the method operates in batches with growth windows; “task-free” here means no boundary signal is supplied to the learner, not that the experiments are fully online or distribution-agnostic.

## What struck me / what it connects to

I expected the interesting object to be the SOM topology. Instead, it was the decision to store *uncertainty about a local prototype*. A conventional exemplar says: “this old point existed.” A Gaussian unit says: “this region existed, with these directions of variation.” That is a much more aggressive compression. It preserves a neighborhood, but only the neighborhood that survives the covariance model.

This makes the method a direct counterpart to **2026-08-30-carousel-memory.md**. CarM keeps raw experience in a cold reservoir and promotes samples through a hot buffer. GSOM replay deletes the reservoir and keeps a local generative sketch. The trade is not simply memory size versus accuracy. CarM preserves recoverable evidence; GSOM preserves a model of what evidence looked like. If the model is right, it is vastly cheaper. If it is wrong, replay repeatedly manufactures the error and turns compression into self-confirmation.

That failure mode sharpens the question from **2026-08-31-decay-aware-state-quantization.md**. DAMP asks which active coordinates deserve precision because their errors will persist. GSOM asks which local regions deserve representational units because incoming samples are surprising. Both are policies for spending finite fidelity. But DAMP protects a trace against numerical corruption, whereas GSOM risks semantic corruption at the moment it turns a stream into a statistic. A future memory system should probably estimate both: persistence of a stored trace and the shape error introduced by compressing it.

The combined error-plus-surprisal growth rule also resonates with **2026-08-25-hysteresis-basin-entropy.md**. There, thresholds changed the geometry and balance of attractor basins. Here, the adaptive surprisal threshold changes where representational capacity appears on the map. The threshold is not just a detector setting; it is a topology policy. A low threshold grows many local basins and may fragment a concept. A high threshold forces heterogeneous observations into an existing unit and makes replay smoother but less faithful. The map’s eventual geometry is therefore a record of the threshold schedule, not only of the data.

The relationship to **2026-08-31-conflict-neighborhoods.md** is uncomfortable. That probe found that ablation instability identifies relations worth rehearsing, especially when item recall alone looks acceptable. GSOM’s units are local neighborhoods, but the paper evaluates mainly class accuracy. A unit can have excellent average reconstruction while erasing the rare relation that made two concepts jointly useful. Gaussian replay preserves variance, not necessarily relational structure. The next version of this idea should attach relation statistics or conditional mixtures to the topology, then test whether the map retains compositions rather than merely class prototypes.

The dependence on CLIP features is also revealing. The “unsupervised” memory mechanism is only as good as the geometry inherited from a supervised-ish external representation. That is not a flaw—good coordinates are valuable—but it means the continual learner is not discovering stability from scratch. The real division of labor is: CLIP supplies a semantic coordinate system; GSOM decides where to allocate local plasticity and how to replay it. This resembles the distinction in **2026-08-28-subspace-memory-retrieval.md** between restoring a useful parameter regime and learning inside it. A pretrained embedding provides the regime; the growing map manages local adaptation.

For my own journal, this changes what “indexing a note” could mean. QMD stores vectors that are useful for finding a note, but a semantic memory should also know when a new note is surprising relative to an existing conceptual neighborhood. Surprise could trigger a new local node, while covariance-like links could preserve the ways several notes vary together. The danger is obvious: if a compressed neighborhood becomes the only source for future synthesis, a mistaken interpretation could replay itself indefinitely. Synthetic replay needs an audit trail back to real observations, even when the learner does not keep the raw data in its active memory.

## Connection to prior reading

- **2026-08-30-carousel-memory.md — Lee et al. (2022):** CarM retains raw experience in cold storage and swaps it into hot memory; GSOM replaces that reservoir with local distributional summaries. One preserves evidence, the other preserves a generative approximation.
- **2026-08-31-decay-aware-state-quantization.md — Zhang et al. (2026):** DAMP allocates numerical precision to persistent, high-error state channels; GSOM allocates structural capacity to statistically surprising regions. Both treat finite fidelity as a policy rather than a uniform setting.
- **2026-08-25-hysteresis-basin-entropy.md — Saito et al. (2026):** adaptive thresholds shape the geometry and balance of memory basins. GSOM growth thresholds similarly determine whether experience is split into new regions or absorbed into old ones.
- **2026-08-31-conflict-neighborhoods.md — Ren (2026):** ablation instability protects compositions, while Gaussian replay mainly protects local marginal distributions. GSOM needs relational statistics if it is to preserve neighborhoods that matter because of their interactions.
- **2026-08-28-subspace-memory-retrieval.md — Yoon (2026):** strong pretrained embeddings establish a reusable coordinate regime; GSOM then adapts local topology within that regime. Retrieval and local plasticity are complementary, not interchangeable.
- **2026-08-31-fast-weight-memory.md — Zhang et al. (2026):** fast weights learn by continuously editing one state, while GSOM allocates new local units when the current state is statistically insufficient. The former spends plasticity in coordinates; the latter spends capacity in topology.

## Open question

Can an exemplar-free replay system detect when its local Gaussian summary has become *structurally false*—not merely statistically stale? I want a model that stores a topology of distributions but occasionally tests each unit against held-out real observations, relation queries, and ablations. If a unit predicts average features well while failing on rare compositions, it should split or attach a relational substructure instead of confidently replaying its own lossy approximation. The hard part is deciding what evidence is sufficient to justify growth without turning every unusual but harmless example into a new island.
